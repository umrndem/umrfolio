## Employee and company data

Do not use blur alone as sanitization. Blurred content may be recoverable and
layout/context can still identify a person or organization.

For an approved screenshot:

1. Work from an approved public-safe environment or synthetic blank state.
2. Remove/crop sensitive regions rather than merely covering them.
3. Replace identifiers with unmistakably generic labels only when approval allows
   a synthetic reconstruction.
4. Check browser chrome, filenames, tabs, URLs, tooltips, hover states, hidden
   panels, and image metadata.
5. Export a flattened derivative.
6. Obtain final approval on the exported file.

Do not create realistic fake employee or medical records.

## Screenshot and media sanitization

Before placing a file in `public/`:

- confirm ownership/licensing and publication approval;
- remove all private text/data;
- inspect edges, background windows, notifications, browser UI, and metadata;
- use a descriptive public-safe filename;
- write alt/caption text that does not reintroduce removed details;
- confirm no confidential project exists elsewhere in the image;
- review at 100% zoom;
- have Umar approve professional material.

Everything under `public/` is reachable without authentication.

## Internal URLs and credentials

Search changed text/assets for:

- `http://`, `https://`, IP-like strings, internal domains;
- `token`, `secret`, `password`, `api_key`, `Authorization`;
- database/storage/provider names and IDs;
- `.env` or command output.

External public URLs must be deliberate and HTTPS. Internal URLs must never be
“hidden” with CSS or omitted from navigation while remaining in HTML/assets.
